The pitch for a selling platform is that it handles the tax, the invoices, the
delivery and the refunds, and that this is worth thirty percent. Three of those
four are worth something. The fourth is worth about two afternoons.

Delivery is a file behind a link that expires. Invoices are a numbered sequence
with no gaps and a frozen copy of who sold what to whom. Refunds are a webhook
and a credit note. None of it is hard; it is only unforgiving, in the way that
anything with a tax authority behind it is unforgiving.

What the platform genuinely sells is not the software. It is the answer to the
question "who is liable" — and on some platforms the answer is genuinely "we
are", because they sell to you and then to the reader. That is worth money. On
others the answer is "you are, we just take the cut", and that is worth
considerably less than they charge.

Read which one you are on before you decide it is not worth doing yourself.
