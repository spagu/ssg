Most advice about writing quotas is written by people who hit one for six weeks
and then wrote a book about it. This one is by someone on her ninth year.

The method is unremarkable and that is the point: a fixed hour, a visible count,
a rule for what happens on the days it does not work. The chapters that matter
are the ones about the days it does not work — the fortnight after a deadline,
the week a project dies, the month a family needs you more than a manuscript
does.

### What is in it

* The three counts worth keeping, and the four that only make you anxious.
* A schedule that survives a job, which most published schedules do not.
* What to do with a draft you have stopped believing in on day eleven.
* Eleven interviews with writers who keep a quota, including two who gave up on
  one and explain why that was the right call.

### What is not in it

No app recommendations, no productivity system with a name, and no chapter on
morning routines. The hour you have is the hour you have.
